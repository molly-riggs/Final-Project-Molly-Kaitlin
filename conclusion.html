Conclusion

Answer to Research Questions

After conducting our research, we have found that social media usage in college-age students has both positive and negative effects on mental health. There are several positive effects, such as improving feelings of connection, support, and self-expression. However, there are several downsides, such as increased anxiety, stress, and comparison when used excessively. Overall, the impact social media has on college students is dependent on how social media is used and how often it is used. We have found that heavy usage tends to come with increased comparison to others online, which results in more negative outcomes.

Why It Matters

Social media is a daily part of student life, making its impact far from insignificant. Because it affects such a large population, its influence on mental health cannot be overlooked. Without increased awareness and intentional action, these effects may continue to worsen over time. Many students do not fully recognize how their social media habits influence their thoughts, behaviors, and overall well-being. This project aims to bring attention to those habits and encourage greater self-awareness, which is the first step toward meaningful change.

With increased awareness, students may be more likely to adopt healthier habits, such as reducing overall usage, engaging more intentionally rather than passively scrolling, and becoming more mindful of comparison triggers. While this project does not seek to dictate behavior, it encourages a more conscious and balanced approach to social media use.

The mental health impact of social media is both real and significant. Issues such as anxiety, stress, and low self-esteem are closely associated with patterns of social media use. These connections are not based solely on opinion, but are supported by both existing research and our own findings.

Additionally, these insights extend beyond individual users and may be valuable to a broader audience, including colleges, counselors, and parents. Understanding these patterns is crucial in helping students develop healthier relationships with social media before negative effects intensify over time.
