Research Methodology

Approach

Our project uses a mixed-method approach that combines both qualitative understanding and quantitative data analysis. The goal we had with our research approach is to better understand how social media usage impacts specifically college students’ mental health through the examination of numerical trends and student perspectives.

To do this, we used a survey as our primary method of data collection to gather quantitative information about social media usage patterns, behaviors, and platform references. We also used secondary research from different academic studies to provide qualitative context and support our data interpretations.

Combining these methods allowed our project to connect real experiences from students with previously existing research, which created a well-rounded understanding of the relationship between social media usage in that age group and mental health.

Our Process

Data Collection

To gather primary data, we distributed a survey to college students regarding their social media use, receiving a total of 22 responses. The survey focused on three main areas: frequency of use, comparison behaviors, and platforms used. Questions related to frequency examined how often students engage with social media on a daily basis. Comparison-based questions explored how frequently students compare themselves to others online, while platform-based questions identified which social media applications are used most often.

We chose a survey as our primary method because it allows for direct insight from students, capturing real behaviors rather than relying solely on theoretical research. Additionally, it provided an efficient way to collect multiple perspectives within a short period of time. This method aligns closely with our research question, as it helps us better understand real usage patterns and provides context for how social media may impact students’ mental health.

To maintain data integrity, participation in the survey was voluntary, ensuring that responses were provided willingly and without external pressure.

Data Preparation

After we sent out our survey, we organized and cleaned up our data using different graphics and visuals. These visuals helped us identify the patterns more clearly, since similar responses were grouped. After we grouped all of the data, we converted the raw data into percentages, so it was easier to understand and looked cleaner on the presentation. The goal of our data collection was to understand different behavioral patterns that college students displayed when it came to social media usage, and the effects that social media usage had on them. This process ensured consistency across responses and allowed us to more effectively identify meaningful patterns and trends within the data.

Tools Used

A variety of digital tools were used throughout this project to support data collection, analysis, and presentation. Each tool served a specific purpose in helping bring the project together.

Google Forms was used to create and distribute the survey to participants. After collecting responses, the platform automatically organized the data and generated initial visual summaries, such as pie charts, which provided a helpful starting point for analysis. This made it an efficient tool for both collecting and organizing responses in one place.

For further analysis, Microsoft Excel was used to organize the survey data, convert responses into percentages, and identify patterns and trends. Excel also allowed for data cleaning, such as removing or correcting any invalid or inconsistent responses, ensuring the accuracy of the dataset.

To expand on the visual analysis, Tableau was used to create more detailed charts and graphs. By importing the Excel dataset into Tableau, we were able to generate clearer and more dynamic visualizations, making our findings easier to interpret and present.

Finally, our website serves as the platform for presenting the final project. It integrates both the written analysis and visual elements, creating a cohesive and engaging experience for the audience. Overall, these tools worked together to support each stage of the project, helping to ensure that the data was collected efficiently, analyzed accurately, and presented clearly.

Analysis Process

Our analysis process had several structured steps. Firstly, all survey responses we collected were through Google Forms and then exported into a spreadsheet. Then, we cleaned and organized the data to ensure consistency and identify patterns in the responses. After this, we calculated percentages for the questions asked, such as frequency of social media use and feelings of comparison. After we identified the trends, we compared our new findings to existing research in the literature review to determine whether our results aligned or differed from previous studies. Lastly, we identified the most significant patterns in our research and showed these through visualizations in the final presentation.

AI Tool Usage

AI tools, specifically ChatGPT, were used to support certain aspects of this project. The tool was primarily used for brainstorming ideas, checking grammar, and assisting with the overall structure of the writing. It helped guide the process by keeping the content organized and cohesive. Additionally, we utilized the built-in chatbot within Visual Studio Code to help debug issues and assist with coding-related questions. In all cases, we ensured that we fully understood any code that was suggested before implementing it.

However, AI tools were not used for data collection, data analysis, or interpreting results. These components were completed independently using our survey data and individual analysis. We also did not rely on AI to generate code that we did not fully understand or verify ourselves.

Overall, AI was used as a supportive tool rather than a replacement for our work. It improved efficiency, helped overcome writer’s block, and assisted in organizing ideas more effectively. While it was also helpful in troubleshooting coding errors, this project still required significant critical thinking, decision-making, and hands-on analysis. Ultimately, the core insights and conclusions of this project were developed through our own understanding and interpretation.

Limitations

This study has several limitations that we considered while interpreting the results. Firstly, the sample size is small, with only 22 responses, which limits how applicable the findings can be to the broader population of college students. Next, the data is also self-reported, so some responses could have been influenced by the memory bias of the respondent. Because of this, participants may not have accurately reflected their behaviors. Lastly, the study identifies a correlation between social media use and mental health, but doesn’t directly show causation. Because of this, we can’t make any entirely definitive conclusions.
